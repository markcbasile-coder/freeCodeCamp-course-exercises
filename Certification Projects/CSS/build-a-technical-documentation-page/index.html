Introduction

Welcome to the core rules reference for Dungeons & Dragons. Whether you are stepping into a dungeon for the first time or brushing up on mechanics between sessions, this documentation outlines the fundamental systems required to play the game.

Dungeons & Dragons is a tabletop roleplaying game driven by dynamic narrative and strategic decision-making. The game relies on a collaborative framework: the Dungeon Master (DM) crafts the world, controls monsters, and sets challenges, while the players create unique characters to explore that world. When outcomes are uncertain, the rules provided in these sections govern how actions are resolved, how combat flows, and how environmental factors impact your journey.

Core Rules

Dungeons & Dragons is a cooperative tabletop roleplaying game where players create characters and embark on adventures narrated by the Dungeon Master (DM). The gameplay follows a rhythmic loop of descriptions and actions:

  1. The DM describes the environment. The DM sets the scene, detailing where the adventurers are and what surrounds them.
  2. The players describe what they want to do. Players declare actions for their characters, whether searching a room, talking to an NPC, or swinging a weapon.
  3. The DM narrates the results of the adventurers' actions. The DM determines if the action succeeds, often calling for a die roll to resolve uncertainty.

Players rely on their character sheet, which outlines core statistics, proficiencies, equipment, and health. The six primary ability scores—Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma—define a character's capabilities and determine modifiers added to d20 rolls.

How Dice Work

When the outcome of an action is uncertain, the game relies on polyhedral dice to determine success or failure. The most important die in the game is the twenty-sided die (d20).

  • Ability Checks: Used when a character attempts a task that requires effort or skill, such as picking a lock or leaping across a chasm.
  • Saving Throws: Used when a character attempts to resist or avoid an incoming hazard, such as dodging a fireball or resisting poison.
  • Attack Rolls: Used during combat to determine whether an attack strikes a target's Armor Class (AC).
The Core Mechanic

To resolve any d20 roll, roll a 20-sided die, add the appropriate ability modifier and relevant proficiency bonus, and compare the total to a target number set by the DM:

  • Difficulty Class (DC): The target number for ability checks and saving throws.
  • Armor Class (AC): The target number required to hit a target with an attack.

If the result equals or exceeds the target number, the attempt succeeds. Rolling a natural 20 on an attack roll automatically hits and deals critical damage, while a natural 1 automatically misses.

Conditions

Conditions alter a creature's capabilities in specific ways and can be applied by spell effects, creature abilities, or environmental hazards.

  • Blinded: A Blinded creature can't see and automatically fails any ability check that requires sight. Attack rolls against the creature have advantage, and the creature's attack rolls have disadvantage.
  • Charmed: A Charmed creature can't attack the charmer or target the charmer with harmful abilities or magical effects. The charmer has advantage on any ability check to interact socially with the creature.
  • Frightened: A Frightened creature has disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls while the source of its fear is within line of sight. The creature can't willingly move closer to the source of its fear.
  • Paralyzed: A Paralyzed creature is incapacitated and can't move or speak. The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws. Attack rolls against the creature have advantage, and any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 5 feet.
  • Prone: A Prone creature's only movement option is to crawl. The creature has disadvantage on attack rolls. An attack roll against the creature has advantage if the attacker is within 5 feet; otherwise, the attack roll has disadvantage.
Movement

During exploration or combat, a creature can move up to a distance equal to its Speed attribute (typically 30 feet for most humanoids).

  • Difficult Terrain: Moving through difficult terrain—such as rubble, deep snow, steep stairs, or dense undergrowth—costs double movement (2 feet of speed for every 1 foot traveled).
  • Interruption and Combination: Movement can be broken up before, during, and after actions on a turn.
  • Special Movement Types:
    • Climbing and Swimming: Each foot of movement costs 1 extra foot (2 extra feet in difficult terrain) unless the creature has a specific climbing or swimming speed.
    • Jumping: A long jump allows travel across a distance up to the character's Strength score if they move 10 feet prior. A high jump clears a height equal to 3 + Strength modifier feet with a 10-foot running start.
  • Standing from Prone: Standing up from a Prone position costs half of a creature's total movement speed for the turn.
Combat

Combat is structured in turns and rounds. Each round represents approximately 6 seconds of in-game time.

  1. Roll Initiative: At the start of combat, every participant rolls a d20 and adds their Dexterity modifier. Turn order proceeds from highest initiative to lowest.
  2. Take a Turn: On your turn, you can move a distance up to your speed and take one Action, one Bonus Action (if granted by a feature or spell), and one Reaction (triggered by specific events during any turn in the round).
Common Actions in Combat
  • Attack: Make a melee or ranged attack against a target.
  • Cast a Spell: Cast a spell with a casting time of 1 action.
  • Dash: Gain extra movement for the current turn equal to your Speed.
  • Disengage: Your movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks for the rest of the turn.
  • Dodge: Focus entirely on avoiding attacks. Attack rolls against you have disadvantage if you can see the attacker, and you make Dexterity saving throws with advantage.
  • Help: Give an ally advantage on their next ability check or attack roll.
  • Hide: Make a Dexterity (Stealth) check to conceal yourself.
